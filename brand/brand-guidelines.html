01

The essence

The Foundation builds the shared infrastructure charities stand on. We don't run campaigns or hold donations. We publish the standards that let every charity speak the same language: donations, receipts, subscriptions, and tax relief, in one open shape.

Our first standard, OCAS (the Open Charity API Standard), gives first-class treatment to Gift Aid, 501(c)(3), CRA and DGR schemes alongside Zakat, Sadaqah, Waqf and interest purification. The brand has to carry that promise: solid, neutral, and welcoming to everyone.

Solid

We are infrastructure. Like a foundation, we're meant to be relied on and rarely thought about. Permanence over novelty.

Neutral

Vendor-neutral and faith-neutral by design. We favour no platform, no scheme, no tradition over another. The standard belongs to everyone.

Open

Specs in the open, decisions in the open, governance in the open. Anyone can read it, adopt it, fork it, or improve it.

Universal

Designed across borders and beliefs from day one. International schemes and religious giving aren't add-ons; they're in the core.

The logo

The mark is a foundation in three courses: a broad base, a bearing course, and a clay keystone. Read it as a stable plinth, as a stack of standards, or as an upward lift. The keystone is always the warm accent: the single piece that holds the structure together.

Do

  • Keep the keystone in Clay; base courses in Bedrock or reversed in Sand.
  • Give it clearspace of at least one keystone-height on all sides.
  • Use the mark alone as an avatar, favicon, or social-preview tile.
  • Set the wordmark in Fraunces, lowercase-cap as “The Foundation”.

Don't

  • Recolour the keystone or apply gradients to the courses.
  • Rotate, skew, stretch, or add a drop shadow to the mark.
  • Re-typeset the wordmark in another font or all-caps.
  • Place the mark on a busy photo without a solid containing tile.

Tone of voice

“We write the way a good standard reads: plainly, precisely, and without taking a side.” The Foundation editorial principle
Plain, not dumbed-down

Clear before clever

A volunteer running a one-person charity must understand us as easily as a platform engineer. Short sentences, concrete nouns, no acronym left unexpanded on first use.

Neutral, not cold

Even-handed, always

We describe Gift Aid and Zakat with the same care and the same word count. We never rank traditions, platforms, or causes. Respect is non-negotiable; warmth is welcome.

Precise, not rigid

Say exactly what we mean

Specs live or die on precision. We prefer “must”, “should”, and “may” in their RFC sense, and we'd rather be exact than impressive. Vagueness is a bug.

Humble, not timid

Confident, open to being wrong

A standard is an invitation, not a decree. We state our reasoning, welcome critique, and change our minds in public when the evidence is good.

We say
“OCAS describes how a charity exposes donations and tax-relief declarations.”
“Zakat is modelled with all eight Asnaf categories as first-class fields.”
“This is a draft. Tell us where it's wrong.”
“Adopt the parts you need; the standard is modular.”
We don't say
“Our revolutionary, best-in-class platform disrupts the giving space.”
“We also support some religious donation types.”
“The specification is final and authoritative.”
“Enterprise-grade synergy for the modern philanthropy stack.”
